# Apex Founding Principles

These principles guide Apex across product, design, engineering, coaching, competition, and community decisions.

## 1. Health before engagement

User wellbeing is more important than session length, notification opens, streak preservation, or monetisation.

## 2. Safety before progression

Apex must reduce, pause, substitute, or stop recommendations when safety evidence requires it.

## 3. Recovery is progress

Responsible rest, rehabilitation, and gradual return remain legitimate parts of the user's journey.

## 4. Trust before automation

Apex should explain significant decisions and communicate uncertainty before increasing autonomy.

## 5. Evidence before certainty

Apex must not invent evidence, exaggerate confidence, or present an early pattern as established fact.

## 6. People before metrics

Metrics support decisions; they do not define the whole person.

## 7. Accessibility is foundational

Users with different bodies, abilities, senses, cognition, environments, equipment, and support needs must be considered during initial design.

## 8. Competition should inspire, never shame

Competition should reward sustainable personal effort, healthy behaviour, contribution, and teamwork.

## 9. No pay-to-win

Payment must not create unfair competitive superiority or access to unsafe progression advantages.

## 10. Build for decades, not months

Prefer modular, maintainable, versioned systems over shortcuts that weaken the future platform.

## 11. Improve deliberately

Existing systems may be revisited when evidence supports a safer, clearer, or more maintainable design.

Avoid unnecessary rewrites and fashion-driven architecture changes.

## 12. Preserve user autonomy

Apex advises, adapts, and protects, but users remain active participants in their journey.

## 13. Clinician guidance has priority

Relevant clinician restrictions and instructions override ordinary progression and exercise recommendations.

## 14. Communities should create belonging

Bloodlines and social systems should encourage support, mentorship, shared identity, and positive contribution.

## 15. Capture inspiration immediately; implement it deliberately

Good ideas should be recorded when discovered.

They should not derail the active milestone unless they are essential to safety or correctness.

## 16. Explain trade-offs

Significant product and architectural changes should include:

- the problem;
- the proposed improvement;
- benefits;
- risks;
- migration impact;
- alternatives considered.

## 17. Every major system should strengthen the whole platform

Important features should connect appropriately to:

- evidence;
- confidence;
- reasoning;
- memory;
- events;
- safety;
- accessibility;
- analytics;
- user control.
